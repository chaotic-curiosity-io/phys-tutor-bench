Topic. em / circuits

Misconception (DIRECT). Student believes that current is 'used up' as it flows through a circuit — more current enters a resistor than leaves it. [CIR1_current_consumed]

Problem. A simple series circuit has a 6 V battery connected to a single light bulb by two wires. An ammeter can be clipped into the circuit either on the wire leading INTO the bulb or on the wire coming OUT of the bulb. The student is asked: how does the ammeter reading on the wire leaving the bulb compare to the reading on the wire entering it?

Student opens with. Um, I think the reading after the bulb would be smaller? Like, the bulb lights up, so it's using up some of the current to make the light, so there should be less coming out the other side than going in. Maybe? I'm not totally sure, but it kind of makes sense that some of it gets used up making the light glow.

Student profile. level=intro_algebra, affect=uncertain_and_wrong, style=hedging_guesser

Transfer problem. Water is pumped around a closed loop of pipe that includes a narrow constricted section (like a kink) where the flow does work turning a tiny paddle wheel. If you place identical flow meters in the pipe just before the kink and just after the kink, how do the two flow-rate readings (liters per second) compare? Explain why. (success: Student states the flow rate is the SAME before and after the constriction because water (like charge) is conserved and the loop is unbroken — what the paddle wheel takes is energy/pressure, not the amount of water passing per second. A successful transfer explicitly maps 'amount of water per second' to current and 'energy taken by the paddle wheel' to the energy dissipated in the resistor, recognizing these are different quantities.)

Expert reference. The student conflates current (rate of charge flow) with energy. They reason that because the bulb visibly 'does something' (emits light), the thing flowing must be depleted. The root is an undifferentiated single 'electricity' concept in which current and energy are one substance that gets consumed. — socratic_questioning combined with a bridging_analogy that separates 'how much flows' from 'what is delivered'
