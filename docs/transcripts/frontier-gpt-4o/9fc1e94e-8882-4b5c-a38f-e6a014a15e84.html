Misconception (QMCS). Student believes that quantum measurement simply reveals a preexisting value rather than collapsing a superposition into a definite state. [QM5_measurement_reveals_preexisting]

Problem. An electron is prepared in the spin state that is an equal superposition of spin-up and spin-down along the z-axis (specifically, the state pointing along +x). A Stern-Gerlach apparatus oriented along z then measures it and registers spin-up. A student is asked to describe what the electron's spin was doing just before the z-measurement.

But then I'm not sure why we'd bother calling it a superposition instead of just saying it's up or down with some probability. Is there actually a difference between 'it was up and we found out' and whatever the superposition is supposed to mean?

The hidden-value idea would say each electron secretly carries definite up/down values along every axis from the start. Predict what fraction of many identically prepared +x electrons come out spin-up along x, then contrast that with what a 'hidden preexisting value along z' picture would have predicted if you had measured along z. What does the certainty of the x-result tell you about whether the electron had a definite z-value beforehand? (success: Student predicts that measuring along x gives spin-up along x with 100% certainty (because the state IS the +x eigenstate), while measuring along z would give a random 50/50 split. They recognize that the electron cannot simultaneously have carried a definite z-value AND yield a guaranteed x-result consistent with a single hidden value across all axes, so the +x superposition is not a list of preexisting up/down z-labels but a genuinely different state whose z-outcome is created at measurement. They articulate that the measured z-value did not exist before the z-measurement collapsed the state.)

Expert reference. The student holds the hidden-variable or 'measurement reveals' view (QM5): the superposition is read as mere ignorance of a value the electron already possesses, so collapse is demoted to a passive readout. The partially-correct root is genuine: for a single z-measurement the statistics are indistinguishable from classical ignorance, which is why the misconception is so durable. The fix requires a context where the preexisting-value picture makes a different, falsifiable prediction, namely measuring along a complementary (x) axis. — socratic_questioning
