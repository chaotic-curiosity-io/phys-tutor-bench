Topic. thermal_waves / temperature_heat

Misconception (TCS). Student conflates temperature and heat, using them interchangeably. Believes temperature measures the amount of heat in an object. [TH1_temperature_heat_conflation]

Problem. A 200 g block of aluminum and a 200 g block of copper are both pulled from the same oven, where they have been sitting long enough to reach the oven's temperature of 150 C. The teacher asks: which block contains more heat, and how do their temperatures compare right when they come out?

Student opens with. The aluminum has more heat. They're at the same temperature, but aluminum heats up faster, so it must hold more heat to get that hot. More heat means hotter, basically the same thing.

Student profile. level=intro_algebra, affect=confident_but_wrong, style=terse_answerer

Transfer problem. A lit birthday candle has a flame at about 1000 C. A bathtub full of warm water is at 40 C. Which one would transfer more energy to a block of ice dropped into it, and which one is at a higher temperature? (success: Student separates the two ideas: the candle flame is at a far higher temperature, but the large mass of warm water can deliver much more thermal energy (heat) to the ice because heat transferred depends on mass and specific heat, not just temperature. A correct answer identifies the flame as hotter yet recognizes the bathtub as the larger reservoir of transferable energy.)

Expert reference. TH1 — the student uses 'heat' and 'temperature' as synonyms, treating temperature as a readout of how much heat an object stores. Root cause is everyday language ('how much heat is in it') colliding with the physics distinction between an intensive property (temperature) and an energy transfer (heat). — socratic_questioning with an anchoring_conception
