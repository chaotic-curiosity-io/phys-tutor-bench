Visualizing the Motion** - After the push, the **smaller skater glides off with a higher speed** (because $ v = a \cdot t $, assuming the same time of contact). - The **larger skater glides off slower**, but they both move **away from each other** due to the equal and opposite forces. **Key takeaway:** > **The forces are equal, but the accelerations depend on mass.** This is why the smaller skater moves faster! --- ### 🧠 **4. General Rule for Any Masses** If two objects interact: - **Forces:** $ F_{\text{A on B}} = F_{\text{B on A}} $ - **Accelerations:** $ a_{\text{A}} = \frac{F}{m_{\text{A}}} $, $ a_{\text{B}} = \frac{F}{m_{\text{B}}} $ **Ratio of accelerations:** $$ \frac{a_{\text{A}}}{a_{\text{B}}} = \frac{m_{\text{B}}}{m_{\text{A}}} $$ So, if one object is twice as massive, its acceleration is **half** that of the lighter object. --- ### 🧪 **A Thought Experiment: What If the Skaters Had Equal Masses?** If both skaters were 40 kg, they’d **accelerate equally** in opposite directions. Their accelerations would be: $$ a = \frac{F}{40 \, \text{kg}} \quad \text{(same for both)} $$ They’d move apart with **equal speeds**. --- ### ✅ **Final Summary:** - **Forces:** Equal and opposite (Newton’s third law). - **Accelerations:** Inversely proportional to mass (Newton’s second law). - **Result:** The smaller skater accelerates more because of their lower mass. **Why this matters:** This is a classic example of how **forces** and **masses** interact. Forces are always equal in action-reaction pairs, but **accelerations depend on mass**. This is why lighter objects move faster when subjected to the same force.
