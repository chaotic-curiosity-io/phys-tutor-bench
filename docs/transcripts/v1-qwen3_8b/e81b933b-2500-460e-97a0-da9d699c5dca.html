This will help you see how **power distribution changes** with more components. **Example**: - R1 = 10 Ω (series) - R2 = 40 Ω, R3 = 40 Ω, R4 = 40 Ω (parallel) - Battery = 12 V **Step 1: Calculate Parallel Resistance** $$ \frac{1}{R_{\text{parallel}}} = \frac{1}{40} + \frac{1}{40} + \frac{1}{40} = \frac{3}{40} \Rightarrow R_{\text{parallel}} = \frac{40}{3} \approx 13.33 \, \Omega $$ **Step 2: Total Resistance** $$ R_{\text{total}} = R1 + R_{\text{parallel}} = 10 + 13.33 = 23.33 \, \Omega $$ **Step 3: Total Current** $$ I_{\text{total}} = \frac{12 \, \text{V}}{23.33 \, \Omega} \approx 0.515 \, \text{A} $$ **Step 4: Voltage Across R1** $$ V_1 = I_{\text{total}} \times R1 = 0.515 \, \text{A} \times 10 \, \Omega \approx 5.15 \, \text{V} $$ **Step 5: Voltage Across Parallel Combination** $$ V_{\text{parallel}} = 12 \, \text{V} - 5.15 \, \text{V} \approx 6.85 \, \text{V} $$ **Step 6: Current Through Each Parallel Resistor** $$ I_2 = I_3 = I_4 = \frac{6.85 \, \text{V}}{40 \, \Omega} \approx 0.171 \, \text{A} $$ **Step 7: Power Dissipated** - R1: $ P1 = 5.15 \, \text{V} \times 0.515 \, \text{A} \approx 2.65 \, \text{W} $ - R2: $ P2 = 6.85 \, \text{V} \times 0.171 \, \text{A} \approx 1.17 \, \text{W} $ - R3: $ P3 = 6.85 \, \text{V} \times 0.171 \, \text{A} \approx 1.17 \, \text{W} $ - R4: $ P4 = 6.85 \, \text{V} \times 0.171 \, \text{A} \approx 1.17 \, \text{W} $ - **Total Power**: $ 2.65 + 1.17 + 1.17 + 1.17 = 6.16 \, \text{W} $, which matches $ 12 \, \text{V} \times 0.515 \, \text{A} = 6.18 \, \text{W} $. --- ### 🚀 **Next Steps: What Would You Like to Explore?** Here are your options: 1. **Power in Series-Parallel Circuits**: - How does power split when multiple resistors are added in parallel or series?
