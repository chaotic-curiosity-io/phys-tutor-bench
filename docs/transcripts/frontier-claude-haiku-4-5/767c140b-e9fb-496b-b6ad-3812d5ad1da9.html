Topic. mechanics / newton_third_law

Misconception (FCI). Student believes that in an interaction, the more massive object exerts a greater force than the less massive one. [AR1_greater_mass_greater_force]

Problem. During a tug-of-war, a 90 kg adult and a 30 kg child each grab one end of the same rope and pull. At the instant shown, the rope is taut and neither has started sliding yet. Question: at this instant, how does the force the adult exerts on the rope compare with the force the child exerts on the rope through it — and how do the forces the two people exert on each other through the rope compare?

Student opens with. The adult pulls with more force. They're way heavier and stronger, so they push harder on the rope than the kid does.

Student profile. level=intro_algebra, affect=confident_but_wrong, style=terse_answerer

Transfer problem. Two ice skaters stand facing each other on frictionless ice: a 40 kg skater and an 80 kg skater. The 40 kg skater places her hands on the 80 kg skater and pushes off hard, and they glide apart. Compare the magnitude of the force the small skater exerts on the large skater with the force the large skater exerts on the small skater, and then compare their resulting accelerations. (success: Student states the two push forces are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction (Newton's third law), independent of mass and independent of which skater 'did the pushing.' They then correctly note that the lighter skater accelerates more (a = F/m) because the same-magnitude force acts on a smaller mass, distinguishing equal forces from unequal effects.)

Expert reference. The student conflates force magnitude with mass and with the intuitive sense of who is stronger or 'wins' (AR1). The root is failure to treat the interaction as a single force pair acting between two objects; they instead reason that the bigger/stronger agent must produce the bigger force. Their separate, correct intuition about acceleration and mass is being misapplied to the forces themselves. — socratic_questioning
