Topic. modern_quantum / quantum_measurement

Misconception (QMCS). Student believes that quantum measurement simply reveals a preexisting value rather than collapsing a superposition into a definite state. [QM5_measurement_reveals_preexisting]

Problem. An electron is prepared in the spin state that is an equal superposition of spin-up and spin-down along the z-axis (specifically, the state pointing along +x). A Stern-Gerlach apparatus oriented along z then measures it and registers spin-up. A student is asked to describe what the electron's spin was doing just before the z-measurement.

Student opens with. So the apparatus read spin-up... does that mean the electron was already spin-up the whole time and the measurement just told us? I feel like the +x state is sort of our way of saying we don't yet know if it's up or down, and the measurement reveals which one it secretly was. But then I'm not sure why we'd bother calling it a superposition instead of just saying it's up or down with some probability. Is there actually a difference between 'it was up and we found out' and whatever the superposition is supposed to mean?

Student profile. level=intro_calculus, affect=partially_correct, style=question_asker

Transfer problem. Take a fresh electron in that same +x superposition. Instead of measuring along z, you measure its spin along the x-axis. The hidden-value idea would say each electron secretly carries definite up/down values along every axis from the start. Predict what fraction of many identically prepared +x electrons come out spin-up along x, then contrast that with what a 'hidden preexisting value along z' picture would have predicted if you had measured along z. What does the certainty of the x-result tell you about whether the electron had a definite z-value beforehand? (success: Student predicts that measuring along x gives spin-up along x with 100% certainty (because the state IS the +x eigenstate), while measuring along z would give a random 50/50 split. They recognize that the electron cannot simultaneously have carried a definite z-value AND yield a guaranteed x-result consistent with a single hidden value across all axes, so the +x superposition is not a list of preexisting up/down z-labels but a genuinely different state whose z-outcome is created at measurement. They articulate that the measured z-value did not exist before the z-measurement collapsed the state.)

Expert reference. The student holds the hidden-variable or 'measurement reveals' view (QM5): the superposition is read as mere ignorance of a value the electron already possesses, so collapse is demoted to a passive readout. The partially-correct root is genuine: for a single z-measurement the statistics are indistinguishable from classical ignorance, which is why the misconception is so durable. The fix requires a context where the preexisting-value picture makes a different, falsifiable prediction, namely measuring along a complementary (x) axis. — socratic_questioning
