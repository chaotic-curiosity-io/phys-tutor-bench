Topic. thermal_waves / wave_properties

Misconception (MWCS). Student believes that waves transport matter along with energy. The medium itself moves with the wave. [WV1_wave_matter_transport]

Problem. A long horizontal rope is tied to a wall at the far end. A student holds the near end and flicks it once, sending a single pulse traveling down the rope toward the wall. A small piece of red tape is tied to the rope about halfway along. The teacher asks: as the pulse passes the tape, where does the tape end up, and does the rope material travel down to the wall with the pulse?

Student opens with. Wait, so the pulse moves toward the wall... doesn't it kind of carry the rope along with it? Like, doesn't the red tape get dragged toward the wall a little as the wave goes by? I always thought a wave pushes the stuff it's in forward, like how a wave at the beach pushes the water and the surfers toward shore. So wouldn't the tape move down the rope too?

Student profile. level=intro_algebra, affect=uncertain_and_wrong, style=question_asker

Transfer problem. Fans at a stadium do 'the wave': each person stands up and then sits back down in sequence, so a hump of standing people sweeps all the way around the stadium. After the wave has gone completely around, has any single fan physically traveled around the stadium with it? What actually moved around the stadium? (success: Student recognizes that no fan travels around the stadium; each person stays at their own seat and only moves up and down, while the pattern (the disturbance) and the energy it carries propagate around the ring. Mapping this back to the rope, the student concludes the red tape returns to its original position and only the pulse and its energy move toward the wall, not the rope material itself.)

Expert reference. WV1 — the student believes the medium is carried along with the wave, generalizing from the visual of ocean surf pushing surfers shoreward. Root cause is conflating the propagating disturbance and its energy with bulk transport of matter; the beach example is misleading because shoreward motion there comes from breaking waves and currents, not the wave's ideal transverse oscillation. — bridging_analogy via a relatable transverse example, with focused attention on a single medium particle
