Misconception (CSEM). Student conflates electric field with electric force, not understanding that the field exists independently of any test charge. [ES3_electric_field_force_conflation]

Problem. A single isolated +5 nC point charge sits at the origin in otherwise empty space. Point P is located 0.20 m to the right of the charge, and there is nothing physically present at P — no other charge, just empty vacuum. The student is asked: is there an electric field at point P, and if so, what is it?

Student opens with. So the source charge definitely sets up a field, I get that part. The field kind of only exists once there's a charge to feel it, right?

Student profile. level=intro_calculus, affect=partially_correct, style=verbose_reasoner

Transfer problem. Consider Earth's gravitational field at a point 300 km above the ground, where an empty region of space contains no satellite, no astronaut, nothing. Is there a gravitational field at that empty point? If a 2 kg sensor were later placed there, what determines the force it would feel — and was the field already present before the sensor arrived? (success: Student asserts the gravitational field exists at the empty point regardless of whether anything is there, because Earth (the source) establishes g throughout the surrounding space. The force on the 2 kg sensor (F = mg) only appears once mass is placed there, but the field g = F/m was already defined and present. A successful transfer explicitly parallels the electric case: the source establishes a field everywhere, the field is a property of space, and the force is a separate quantity requiring an object to act upon.)

Expert reference. The student has correctly grasped that a source charge creates a field but conflates the field with the force it would exert, so they believe the field 'turns on' only when a test charge is present to feel it. The root is reading E = F/q as 'field requires a force, force requires a charge at the point,' rather than understanding the field as a property the source assigns to every point in space independent of any test charge. — anchoring_conception leveraging the student's correct 'source creates a field' idea, plus socratic_questioning to separate field from force
