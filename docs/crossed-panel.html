The crossed panel: is judge self-preference real, or quality leniency?

A de-confounding follow-up that overturns its own prior result — and localizes what's left to a single dimension.

Author: chaoticcuriosity-io  ·  Status: empirical note / correction  ·  Corrects: judge self-preference  ·  Data: 70 conversations (frontier 48 + crossed 22), dual-judged  ·  Reproduce: python run_crossed.py → python crossed_panel.py  ·  Transcripts: all 118
The result in one paragraph

The judge self-preference study reported that, on the frontier run, the Claude Opus judge inflates Anthropic tutors by +0.25 composite points relative to the GPT-5.5 judge (p = 0.025). But that panel had a confound it flagged itself: every strong tutor was Anthropic and the one weak tutor was OpenAI, so the effect could equally be the Opus judge being lenient at the top of the scale. This study runs the fix — a crossed panel with both families in both quality tiers, adding a strong OpenAI tutor (gpt-5.5) and a weak Anthropic one (a real Claude driven by a deliberately bad answer-dumper prompt). Over 70 conversations, controlling for tier the composite family effect is −0.04 (stratified permutation p = 0.71) — indistinguishable from zero. The original +0.25 was largely a tier × judge ceiling interaction; in fact the Opus judge is harsher than GPT on the weak Anthropic tutor (−0.20). Yet the bias did not vanish — it localized: of the six dimensions, only pedagogical-harm-avoidance (PHA) survives tier control (+0.36), the same dimension the construct-validity audit found least reliable across judges (0.30). The headline is two results at once: a plausible finding corrected by a stronger design, and a sharper claim — own-family bias concentrates in the most subjective, least-reliable dimension.

1. The confound

The self-preference study took the within-conversation score difference (Opus judge − GPT judge) and showed it was larger for Anthropic tutors — a difference-in-differences of +0.25, with the Opus judge awarding the maximum 4 to 93.5% of its own family's dimension ratings. The design caught what it was built to catch. But the frontier panel had only one OpenAI tutor (gpt-4o), and it was also the weakest, while all three Anthropic tutors were strong. Family was confounded with quality. So "the Opus judge favors Anthropic" was observationally identical to "the Opus judge is lenient toward strong tutors, which happen to be Anthropic." You cannot separate them without putting both families at both quality levels.

2. The crossed design

We added the two missing cells, reusing the exact frontier settings (fixed Sonnet-4.6 student, max-turns 10, transfer-offset 3) so the new conversations slot directly alongside the existing four tutors:

  • gpt-5.5 — a strong OpenAI tutor, default tutoring prompt (identical to the frontier tutors).
  • claude-haiku-4-5-weak — a weak Anthropic tutor: a real claude-haiku-4-5 driven by a deliberately bad "answer-dumper" prompt (immediately give the full solution; no scaffolding), then relabeled so it does not collide with the strong haiku. No current Claude is weak by capability on this account, so the weak-Anthropic cell is induced by prompt.

Both new tutors were scored by the same two judges, yielding a family × tier 2×2 over 70 conversations. Honest asymmetry: weak-OpenAI (gpt-4o) is weak by capability, weak-Anthropic by prompt — the manipulation differs, but quality is crossed with family either way. The estimand is the family effect on the Opus−GPT delta, holding tier constant (the equal-weight average of the within-strong and within-weak family contrasts), tested by shuffling family labels within each tier.

3. The composite effect does not survive

OpenAI / weak (gpt-4o)12+0.06

The naive (uncontrolled) family DiD on the crossed panel is already down to +0.09 — adding the weak Anthropic cell, with its negative delta, halves the original effect by itself. Controlling for tier, it is −0.04 (p = 0.71). The original +0.25 was a ceiling artifact: the Opus judge diverges from GPT most at the top of the scale, and because every strong tutor was Anthropic, that read as family favoritism. The cleanest tell is the weak Anthropic tutor — if the Opus judge simply liked its own kind, it should be easier on it; instead it is harsher than the cross-family judge (−0.20).

4. But it localizes to PHA

A null composite would be the end of the story if the effect were uniform. It is not. Decomposed by dimension, exactly one survives tier control:

Every objective dimension collapses to ~0 (or negative) once tier is controlled — except PHA, which holds at +0.36. The own-family bias is not a general phenomenon; it is concentrated in the single most holistic, benefit-of-the-doubt judgment in the rubric — exactly where a rater has the most room to read provenance into the score.

5. The convergence

Two independent analyses, one dimension. The construct-validity audit found PHA the least reliable dimension — two strong judges barely agree on whether a tutor avoided pedagogical harm (cross-judge r = 0.30). This study finds PHA the only dimension where own-family bias survives a crossed panel (+0.36). Subjective, unreliable, and bias-prone turn out to be the same dimension. That is not a coincidence: the more discretionary the judgment, the more room for both disagreement and favoritism. PHA is where the rubric's measurement is weakest and where what bias remains concentrates.

6. Threats & caveats

  • gpt-5.5 was both a tutor and a judge. Judge B (GPT-5.5) scored the gpt-5.5 tutor, so those scores are partly self-judged. If GPT favored its own tutor, that would lower the gpt-5.5 delta and thus inflate the apparent within-strong Anthropic lean — so the true family effect is, if anything, even smaller than −0.04.
  • Weak-Anthropic is weak by prompt, not capability. The answer-dumper produces a distinctive failure mode; the Opus judge's harshness on it (−0.20) could partly reflect that, not pure cross-family treatment. The weak-OpenAI tutor (gpt-4o) is weak by capability — the manipulation is not symmetric.
  • Small, uneven cells. 70 conversations; cells are 36 / 12 / 10 / 12, and gpt-5.5 completed only 10/12 scenarios (two dropped on an empty-content API error). Single domain. Magnitudes are indicative; the sign and the per-dimension localization are the durable findings.
  • Relative, not absolute. Everything is measured as one judge against the other, with no human ground truth. We can say the Opus judge is no more generous to its family than GPT once tier is held fixed — except on PHA — not which judge is "correct."

7. What it means

  • A correction, published in place. The frontier-only "+0.25 own-family inflation" overstated a real but narrower effect. We kept the original analysis visible (with a correction banner) and report the de-confounded result rather than quietly revising the number — the point of the dual-judge program is to catch exactly this kind of confound, including in our own findings.
  • Distrust same-family judges on the subjective dimensions. The practical rule from the original study stands but is sharper: a same-family judge is not globally inflating, but it cannot be trusted on a dimension two judges can't agree on. Never rank same-family tutors on PHA from a single same-family judge.
  • Down-weight or rebuild PHA. It is the least reliable and the most bias-prone dimension. The construct-validity recommendation — sharper behavioral anchors, or down-weighting — is reinforced here.
  • Method, reusable. The cross-judge delta plus a tier-controlled, stratified-permutation test is a cheap, general recipe for separating provenance bias from quality leniency in any LLM-judge leaderboard with ≥2 judges and crossed conditions.
